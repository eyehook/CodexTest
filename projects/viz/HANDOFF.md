# Nacre

Open `/projects/viz/` on the [local preview server](../../AGENTS.md). Begin listening on desktop or choose Enter VR on Quest. Pause or resume with the desktop button or either controller trigger. Push either thumbstick right (then return toward neutral) or choose Next song on desktop to skip to the following song. Inputs held during tracking/session entry are absorbed. Skipping works while paused without resuming playback. Each load draws a random unsigned 32-bit seed and displays it in the Seed field. Enter a number from 0 through 4294967295 and choose Use seed to reopen the chamber at the beginning, then begin playback. A direct link with `?seed=123` also selects a song sequence once. Refresh draws a fresh seed. The desktop readout shows the one-based song number, progression label and shape number. A root seed plus shape number identifies a generated sculpture. To identify a song you dislike, report the root Seed field plus song number (and optionally the progression label); a seed alone identifies the sequence, not which song within it. This expanded progression bank changes melodies generated by existing seeds.

In VR, A (right) or X (left), xr-standard button 4, toggles conductor mode. The activating hand emits a narrow, outward-flowing [particle pointer](particle-pointer.mjs), with no baton or solid ray. Its 96 evenly spaced particles move outward along one central axis with individually seeded angular drift, radial breathing and lateral wander (within about 2.6 cm of the axis) at 0.22 lengths per second (about 4.5 seconds to reach the circles), share one GPU point draw and fade as they approach the note-circle plane (or its distance when aimed away). Five vertically stacked circles appear beside that hand’s forward aiming area, anchored in reference/world space when the mode starts rather than following the baton. Aim at a circle to select one of five ascending active-triad pitches; all circles have a fixed 7.15 cm radius (10% larger than the original), 50% opacity and a color sampled from the sculpture’s current interpolated pearl palette, with no beat pulse. The selected target is an additional 25% larger and brighter. Circle centers remain 18 cm apart. The pointer stream follows the same color. A 72-particle [swirl](note-swirl.mjs) uses individual damped attraction toward moving orbital targets around the selected circle. Different response speeds produce fast leaders, a curling trailing stream and gentle overshoot; new selections redirect existing positions and velocities rather than moving a shared ring. When no circle is selected, attraction weakens, orbits loosen, and the particles slowly fade. It uses one point draw, reusable position/velocity buffers and a bounded 120 Hz substep simulation for 72 particles; only the small position buffer is uploaded per update. The particles use 50% larger point sprites and brighter, broader halos, following the same palette. This targeting feedback follows real time (including while music is paused); mode exit hides and resets it. Aim away to leave upcoming lead onsets silent while accompaniment continues; sounding lead notes finish naturally. Exit conductor mode to restore the original melody. The selection applies only to scheduled lead onsets; it does not add notes, bend pitches or interrupt sounding notes. Bass, pads, secondary voice and drums retain their song events. Either A/X exits the mode and removes the ray/targets. Controller sleep, tracking loss or disconnect preserves the pointer at its last tracked position, direction and reach; its particles continue flowing. Circles, highlight, selection swirl and the last selected pitch remain active. The pointer is attached to the scene root and copies only valid tracked controller world poses, so WebXR hiding/resetting the controller cannot hide or move the retained stream. Reconnection of the same hand restores pointing without moving the circles; held A/X is absorbed. Session end returns to song mode. Held buttons are absorbed on entry/recovery. Physical Quest sleep/wake behavior, aiming alignment and target spacing remain to be tested.

A single sculpture with 43 ribbon layers sits at seated eye level, 6.5 m ahead at 1.022× model scale. It turns smoothly around the fixed vertical (Y) axis, completing a revolution every 80–112 beats, with a small positional sway. Analytic quaternion rotation follows the musical clock, so pause, seek and seed replay preserve orientation. A single GPU point draw animates 1,440 motes: kick hits send broad three-dimensional bursts beyond the rim, while lead notes illuminate a drifting cloud around the sculpture. Each cloud particle has its own orbital speed, radial wander and floating phase; pitch applies a staggered vertical attraction instead of translating a rigid ring. A critically damped pitch response preserves momentum through note changes, and a smoothed attraction strength releases the cloud back into floating motion. [Particle motion](particle-motion.mjs) evolves in musical beats and freezes on pause. Larger soft halos, bright cores and square-root response envelopes make the effects stronger and longer-lived; they still fade out during silence. Particles rotate with the sculpture; the star field stays fixed. Scheduled kick onsets expand every form. Bell pitch selects a band of lips to lift and illuminate for the note envelope. Smoothed post-compressor RMS opens the geometry and brightens its rim. A dark star field remains fixed for orientation.

- [Score](score.mjs) maps the root seed and song number to a deterministic song seed. Each song follows intro (one eight-bar phrase), Theme A (two), contrasting Theme B in the same song key (two), varied A (two), bridge (one), climax (remaining two to five), and outro (two). Tempo stays at the root seed’s 78–95 BPM across the sequence; song length is rounded to 12–15 eight-bar phrases nearest five minutes (roughly 4:48–5:08). Themes retain their pitches and rhythms on return, while articulation, groove, density and secondary voice change by section. The intro gradually introduces pads, bass and melodic hints; the bridge drops percussion; the climax adds the secondary synth. The progression catalog includes I–V–vi–IV, vi–IV–I–V, I–vi–IV–V, IV–V–vi–I, I–IV–V–I, I–vi–ii–V and minor-centered i–VI–III–VII. A root-seeded offset/stride visits all seven once per seven songs without adjacent repeats; new song seeds still choose tonic, motifs, sound palette and grooves. Each song retains one progression and key across all sections, with one chord per bar. The final entry uses natural minor; the others use major. The vi-starting major and minor-centered entries share a chord-cycle relationship but establish different tonic/cadence behavior; melodies and conductor choices follow those chords. The last phrase replaces the final two bars with IV–I (iv–i in minor), releases its final tonic, and leaves two beats for tails before the next song starts automatically. Only the current phrase and future lead selections are retained; expired lead records are pruned by the scheduler even while rendering is inactive. Melodies combine clear quarter-note landings with short eighth-note runs and rests. Kick, bass and secondary voice remain on quarter-note positions. A song-seeded choice of straight backbeat, four-on-the-floor or half-time feel changes kick/brush patterns. Hats start with eighths; busier sections add closing sixteenth runs, alternating eighth/sixteenth bars, or two short runs. Sixteenth ghosts are quieter than eighths and beat accents, and dense patterns receive a gain reduction. Eight two-bar melodic patterns retain varied gaps and endings. Rhythm references: [Ableton beat programming](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/programming-beats-2-linear-drumming) and [velocity variation](https://cdn-resources.ableton.com/resources/uploads/makingmusic/MakingMusic_DennisDeSantis.pdf). Three-pass melody generation plans motifs and register arcs, scores four harmonic realizations, then adds sparse stepwise ornaments. See [MeloForm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14345) and [non-chord tones](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/NonChordTones.html).
- [Audio](audio.mjs) schedules sixteenth notes against AudioContext time with 160 ms lookahead. The four sustained pad voices use half gain (−6 dB) before the shared delay. [Voice palettes](voices.mjs) deterministically select a warm analog, hollow electronic or glassy family from dedicated seed lanes. Bass blends a filtered harmonic oscillator with a sine fundamental; lead uses filtered FM plucks; pads pair gently detuned oscillators with slow envelopes; the secondary voice uses a filtered square/triangle. Each instrument varies its cutoff and envelope within bounded ranges, with seeded FM depth/ratio, pad detuning and a restrained echo send. Drums retain synthesized noise and kick pitch envelopes. Notes use at most two oscillators and disconnect their filters, gains and sources when finished. Timbres stay fixed within each song and change at the next song boundary; the root seed reproduces the full sequence of notes and voices. The scheduler switches palettes using the scheduled song index, including across stalls. Manual skip fades/stops active and lookahead-scheduled voices before starting the next song, preserving the AudioContext’s paused/running state. Short existing delay tails may carry across the transition. Suspended audio freezes the transport. After a timer stall, scheduling skips missed onsets. Finished voices disconnect and the scheduler retains only the current phrase.
- [Conductor](conductor.mjs) reuses shared ButtonEdges and ControllerPointer. Controller target-ray groups drive the particle pointer; both target-ray and grip tracking are required to change the selection, but loss of tracking preserves conductor mode and the last selection. Target transforms and controller rays are compared in the same unscaled local-reference/world space. `leadChoices` in the score supplies five chord tones at each onset, in the current major/minor progression. `Loom.setLeadChoice` replaces only future lead voices inside the scheduler lookahead, retaining their original times, envelopes and palette; changing/clearing selection leaves sounding voices untouched and updates the particle cue pitch.
- [Chamber](chamber.mjs) deforms instanced strips using [generated shape plans](shapes.mjs). Every eight-bar period has a fresh deterministic shape, with no fixed forms or seven-shape cycle. A seed-selected family order visits sphere, cone, cylinder, torus, simple trefoil knot, hourglass, bowl, spiral, saddle, figure-eight and Möbius strip once per eleven shapes, with no adjacent family repeats. Every visit generates new dimensions, taper, stretch, orientation and deformation. All families allow wider proportions, asymmetry, bending, twist and opening than the initial primitive generator; the variation is not limited to cones. Cones additionally vary side curvature from straight to flared/concave; hourglasses vary waist and bell flare; bowls vary depth and rim curl; spirals vary winding count and depth with thin, spaced coils; saddles are open bent sheets; figure-eights separate their crossing in depth; Möbius strips carry one half-twist. One to three broad folds keep local detail bounded. Continuous 64-beat opening/unwinding waves vary by the generated plan and across layers. Conservative normalization bounds the base sculpture within 3.2 m of its center before music deformation. Periodic angular terms keep each ring closed. Both generated endpoints are evaluated independently and blended over five beats, keeping transitions smooth without interpolating discrete winding counts. Kick expansion, volume opening, pitch-localized lifting/illumination, vertical rotation and surrounding particle behavior remain applied after shape generation. Plans/uniforms change only at period boundaries; the same 43 strips and draw count are retained. Layer intersections and compact or nearly flat silhouettes can still occur; these are artistic generator outcomes, not collision-aware surfaces. [Application](app.mjs) uses the audio clock for rendering and shared XRFrameState to gate untracked frames.

Desktop Chrome rendered 22 seed-123 sculptures (two visits to each family) for visual inspection and exercised 336 shape/transition/music-response samples across seeds 7, 42 and 123 without shader/runtime errors.
