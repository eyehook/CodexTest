# Nacre

Open `/projects/viz/` on the [local preview server](../../AGENTS.md). Begin listening on desktop or choose Enter VR on Quest. Pause or resume with the desktop button or either controller trigger. Push either thumbstick right (then return toward neutral) or choose Next song on desktop to skip to the following song. Inputs held during tracking/session entry are absorbed. Skipping works while paused without resuming playback. Each load draws a random unsigned 32-bit seed and displays it in the Seed field. Enter a number from 0 through 4294967295 and choose Use seed to reopen the chamber at the beginning, then begin playback. A direct link with `?seed=123` also selects a song sequence once. Refresh draws a fresh seed.

A single sculpture with 43 ribbon layers sits at seated eye level, 6.5 m ahead at 1.022× model scale. It turns smoothly around the fixed vertical (Y) axis, completing a revolution every 80–112 beats, with a small positional sway. Analytic quaternion rotation follows the musical clock, so pause, seek and seed replay preserve orientation. A single GPU point draw animates 1,440 motes: kick hits send broad three-dimensional bursts beyond the rim, while lead notes illuminate a drifting cloud around the sculpture. Each cloud particle has its own orbital speed, radial wander and floating phase; pitch applies a staggered vertical attraction instead of translating a rigid ring. A critically damped pitch response preserves momentum through note changes, and a smoothed attraction strength releases the cloud back into floating motion. [Particle motion](particle-motion.mjs) evolves in musical beats and freezes on pause. Larger soft halos, bright cores and square-root response envelopes make the effects stronger and longer-lived; they still fade out during silence. Particles rotate with the sculpture; the star field stays fixed. Scheduled kick onsets expand every form. Bell pitch selects a band of lips to lift and illuminate for the note envelope. Smoothed post-compressor RMS opens the geometry and brightens its rim. A dark star field remains fixed for orientation.

- [Score](score.mjs) maps the root seed and song number to a deterministic song seed. Each song follows intro (one eight-bar phrase), Theme A (two), contrasting relative-key Theme B (two), varied A (two), bridge (one), climax (remaining two to five), and outro (two). Tempo stays at the root seed’s 78–95 BPM across the sequence; song length is rounded to 12–15 eight-bar phrases nearest five minutes (roughly 4:48–5:08). Themes retain their pitches and rhythms on return, while articulation, groove, density and secondary voice change by section. The intro gradually introduces pads, bass and melodic hints; the bridge drops percussion; the climax adds the secondary synth. The last phrase closes V–I, releases its final tonic, and leaves two beats for tails before the next song starts automatically. Only the current phrase is retained. Three-pass melody generation plans motifs and register arcs, scores four harmonic realizations, then adds sparse stepwise ornaments. See [MeloForm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14345) and [non-chord tones](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/NonChordTones.html).
- [Audio](audio.mjs) schedules sixteenth notes against AudioContext time with 160 ms lookahead. The four sustained pad voices use half gain (−6 dB) before the shared delay. [Voice palettes](voices.mjs) deterministically select a warm analog, hollow electronic or glassy family from dedicated seed lanes. Bass blends a filtered harmonic oscillator with a sine fundamental; lead uses filtered FM plucks; pads pair gently detuned oscillators with slow envelopes; the secondary voice uses a filtered square/triangle. Each instrument varies its cutoff and envelope within bounded ranges, with seeded FM depth/ratio, pad detuning and a restrained echo send. Drums retain synthesized noise and kick pitch envelopes. Notes use at most two oscillators and disconnect their filters, gains and sources when finished. Timbres stay fixed within each song and change at the next song boundary; the root seed reproduces the full sequence of notes and voices. The scheduler switches palettes using the scheduled song index, including across stalls. Manual skip fades/stops active and lookahead-scheduled voices before starting the next song, preserving the AudioContext’s paused/running state. Short existing delay tails may carry across the transition. Suspended audio freezes the transport. After a timer stall, scheduling skips missed onsets. Finished voices disconnect and the scheduler retains only the current phrase.
- [Chamber](chamber.mjs) deforms instanced strips in a vertex shader. Each seven-period movement opens as a shell and visits six forms in seeded order: a spiral bloom, tide, trefoil knot, folded globe, paired wings and hourglass. A seed-offset 64-beat cycle drives form-specific deformation, delayed across layers: shell lips peel out of their cup; the spiral bloom winds and opens its scalloped crown; tide sheets roll into a crest; trefoil loops loosen with a torsion wave; globe latitudes open into a pleated lantern; wings hinge with trailing tips; hourglass bells counter-twist around a breathing waist. The cycle uses periodic functions for seamless wrapping and freezes with the audio transport. The slower decorative clock no longer resets every 4π, avoiding jumps in fractional-frequency motions. All deformation stays in the existing vertex shader, using the same strips and draw call. Geometry, folds, winding and color interpolate over five beats at section changes. [Application](app.mjs) uses the same audio clock for rendering and the shared XRFrameState helper to gate untracked frames.
